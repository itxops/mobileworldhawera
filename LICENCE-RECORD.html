04

The six brand logos

Apple, Samsung, Huawei, Oppo, Vivo and Google appear in the “Brands we repair” row. Two separate things apply here, and they are worth keeping apart.

The icon files — public domain

The six shapes come from Simple Icons, package simple-icons version 13.21.0, released under CC0 1.0 Universal — a public domain dedication. Confirmed from the package manifest on the compile date. There is therefore no copyright question over the files.

The trademarks — not owned, and not claimed

The marks remain the property of Apple Inc., Samsung, Huawei, Oppo, Vivo and Google. This website claims no ownership of them and does not need to: they identify which devices the shop repairs, which is ordinary descriptive use of the kind every independent repair business relies on.

Two design decisions support that position:

  • A disclaimer sits directly beneath the logo row, in the page's own words: “Brand names and logos are shown only to help you identify your device. Mobile World Hawera is an independent repair store and is not an authorised service centre for, or affiliated with, any of these manufacturers.”
  • The logos are drawn in a single flat colour rather than each brand's official colours, which keeps clear of manufacturer brand style guidelines.

If a manufacturer ever objects, removing one takes a single edit in public/index.html: delete both copies of that brand — the visible one and its data-mq-clone twin, which exists only so the row scrolls seamlessly.

The Mobile World Hawera logo

This is the business's own property, supplied by the client as logo.png and kept in the project root as the original source file.

It was traced to vector format so it stays sharp at any size, including print. No colour was altered. The charcoal “M” and “MOBILE”, the red “W” and “WORLD”, and the grey “Hawera” are exactly as supplied, and the counters — the enclosed holes in O, B, R, D, a and e — are properly transparent, so the mark sits correctly on any background. No reversed or recoloured version is used anywhere on the site.

The 26 interface icons — phone, WhatsApp, map pin, envelope, clock, battery, droplet, speaker, signal, tablet, laptop and the rest — were drawn specifically for this website and carry no third-party rights.

08

Verifying any of this

Anyone can check a photograph in well under a minute:

  1. Take any Photo ID from the table in section 3.
  2. Open https://www.pexels.com/photo/<ID>/ in a browser.
  3. The page shows the photograph and the words “Free to use”.
  4. The full terms are linked from every Pexels page at pexels.com/license.

For the brand logos, check the licence stated in the footer at simpleicons.org, or read the package manifest directly at cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/simple-icons@13/package.json — the license field reads CC0-1.0.

09

If a claim is ever received

  1. Do not remove anything immediately. Check the claim against this record first — a claim is not proof.
  2. Identify which image is being claimed, and find its row in section 3.
  3. Reply with the Photo ID and the Pexels source URL, plus a link to the licence. In most cases that ends the matter, because the claimant is looking at a photograph they also found on a free stock site.
  4. If the claim concerns a brand logo rather than a photograph, point to section 4 and to the disclaimer already published beneath the logo row.
  5. If the claim is formal — a letter from a lawyer or a rights-management agency — take this document to a solicitor before responding or paying anything. Speculative invoicing over stock images is common.
  6. Keep this record current. If an image is ever swapped, add its replacement to section 3 with the new Photo ID and source URL.

Scope and limits

Read this before relying on the document.

This record covers only the material on the website as at 18 September 2026. Anything added later must be recorded here too.

Licence terms can change. The Pexels terms in section 2 are quoted as they stood on that date, which is why the date is recorded throughout.

If you replace any stock photo with your own photograph of the shop, note it here as your own work. That is the strongest position of all, and it is recommended for the shopfront image in particular.
