## 4. The six brand logos

Apple, Samsung, Huawei, Oppo, Vivo and Google appear in the "Brands we repair"
row. Two separate things apply here, and they are worth keeping apart.

### The icon files — public domain

The six shapes come from **Simple Icons** (<https://simpleicons.org>), package
`simple-icons` version 13.21.0, released under **CC0 1.0 Universal** — a public
domain dedication. Confirmed from the package manifest on the compile date.
There is therefore no copyright question over the files.

### The trademarks — not owned, and not claimed

The marks remain the property of Apple Inc., Samsung, Huawei, Oppo, Vivo and
Google. This website claims no ownership of them and does not need to: they
identify *which devices the shop repairs*, which is ordinary descriptive use of
the kind every independent repair business relies on.

Two design decisions support that position:

- A disclaimer sits directly beneath the logo row, in the page's own words:
  "Brand names and logos are shown only to help you identify your device. Mobile
  World Hawera is an independent repair store and is not an authorised service
  centre for, or affiliated with, any of these manufacturers."
- The logos are drawn in a single flat colour rather than each brand's official
  colours, which keeps clear of manufacturer brand style guidelines.

If a manufacturer ever objects, removing one takes a single edit in
`public/index.html`: delete **both** copies of that brand — the visible one and
its `data-mq-clone` twin, which exists only so the row scrolls seamlessly.

```html
<li class="brand-chip"><svg …><use href="#i-brand-huawei"/></svg><span>Huawei</span></li>
<li class="brand-chip" data-mq-clone aria-hidden="true">…Huawei…</li>
```

## 6. The Mobile World Hawera logo, and the interface icons

**The logo is the business's own property**, supplied by the client as
`logo.png` and kept in the project root as the original source file.

It was traced to vector format (`public/assets/logo/logo.svg`) so it stays sharp
at any size, including print. **No colour was altered.** The charcoal "M" and
"MOBILE", the red "W" and "WORLD", and the grey "Hawera" are exactly as supplied,
and the counters — the enclosed holes in O, B, R, D, a and e — are properly
transparent, so the mark sits correctly on any background. No reversed or
recoloured version is used anywhere on the site.

The 26 interface icons — phone, WhatsApp, map pin, envelope, clock, battery,
droplet, speaker, signal, tablet, laptop and the rest — were drawn specifically
for this website and are delivered as an inline SVG sprite. They are not taken
from any icon library and carry no third-party rights.

## 7. Record of due diligence

The Pexels Licence covers **copyright**. It does not cover a **trademark that
appears inside a photograph**, which is a separate matter. Every candidate image
was therefore opened and examined at full resolution before use, and anything
showing a manufacturer's name or logo was rejected.

| Rejected | Reason |
|---|---|
| JBL portable speaker | JBL wordmark on the grille |
| Bose speaker | BOSE wordmark across the front |
| Marshall speakers (×2) | Marshall script logo |
| Sony speaker | SONY wordmark |
| MIVI speaker | MIVI wordmark |
| AirPods and earbuds (several) | Distinctive Apple product design and logo |
| Phone cases (several) | Apple logo visible on the cases |
| Phone home screens (several) | Chrome, WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram, Play Store icons |
| A battery repair photo | A laptop full of visible app icons had crept into the frame |

Also excluded as a matter of policy:

- No manufacturer's own product photography
- No image taken from any other repair business, including the reference site
- No image sourced from a search engine or an unidentified website

The Bluetooth speaker that *was* used — photo 13465232 — was checked at full
resolution specifically to confirm it carries no wordmark, only an embossed
volume control.

## 8. Verifying any of this

Anyone can check a photograph in well under a minute:

1. Take any Photo ID from the table in section 3.
2. Open `https://www.pexels.com/photo/<ID>/` in a browser.
3. The page shows the photograph and the words "Free to use".
4. The full terms are linked from every Pexels page at <https://www.pexels.com/license/>.

For the brand logos, check the licence stated in the footer at
<https://simpleicons.org>, or read the package manifest directly at
<https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/simple-icons@13/package.json> — the `license` field
reads `CC0-1.0`.

## 9. If a claim is ever received

1. **Do not remove anything immediately.** Check the claim against this record
   first — a claim is not proof.
2. **Identify which image** is being claimed, and find its row in section 3.
3. **Reply with the Photo ID and the Pexels source URL**, plus a link to the
   licence. In most cases that ends the matter, because the claimant is looking
   at a photograph they also found on a free stock site.
4. **If the claim concerns a brand logo** rather than a photograph, point to
   section 4 and to the disclaimer already published beneath the logo row.
5. **If the claim is formal** — a letter from a lawyer or a rights-management
   agency — take this document to a solicitor before responding or paying
   anything. Speculative invoicing over stock images is common.
6. **Keep this record current.** If an image is ever swapped, add its replacement
   to section 3 with the new Photo ID and source URL.
